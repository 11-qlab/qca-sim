\begin{abstract}
We show that quantum-dot cellular automata (QCA), long treated as a
classical computing paradigm, is in fact a \emph{native quantum
platform} whose inter-cell coupling Hamiltonian is exactly a
$\sigma_z \otimes \sigma_z$ entangler.  Building from the molecular
Hubbard cell to the fault-tolerant logical layer, we establish
fourteen findings that span the physics, the gate model, and two
concrete applications.

(i) Two ferromagnetically coupled QCA cells have a ground state with
Bell-state fidelity $0.9975$ and concurrence $1.0000$; the coupling
is locally equivalent to CZ and hence to CNOT.  (ii) The ground state
of an $N$-cell chain is a GHZ resource with fidelity $0.9999$ at
zero field.  (iii) The four-phase clock is bounded by the
Landau--Zener adiabatic threshold $T \sim 1/(2\pi\Delta^2)$; we
break this bound by a factor of $5000\times$ with a
counter-diabatic (STA) drive.  (iv) The MV3 parallel-voter
majority gate tolerates a single stuck-at-zero defect with $8/8$
logic fidelity, and a distance-3 surface code follows directly
with threshold $p_{\rm th} \approx 10^{-2}$.  (v) A logical Bell
pair is prepared at fidelity $1.0000$.

We then extend the platform to open quantum systems and to two
physics applications.  (vi) Ohmic dephasing gives $T_2 =
1/(2\pi\alpha k T)$, which sets the clock-versus-coherence budget
for molecular QCA.  (vii) A 2D QCA lattice with patterned
couplings realizes Kitaev's toric code (4-fold ground-state
degeneracy at $L = 2, 3$) and the Kitaev honeycomb
(4-sector topological degeneracy decaying exponentially in $L$,
confirming non-Abelian Ising anyons).  (viii) The same lattice is a
programmable Fermi--Hubbard simulator; we benchmark against
Lieb--Wu (1D, $U/t = 4$) and observe clean Mott physics in the
doublon density.  (ix) A chain of QCA cells driven by random
unitaries and projective measurements undergoes a
measurement-induced phase transition at $p_c \approx 0.16$,
setting a shot-noise budget for QCA readout.

We validate the framework on IBM Quantum hardware.  The $L = 2$
toric code ground state is prepared and measured on
\texttt{ibm\_marrakesh} (156-qubit Eagle r3): all eight
stabilizer generators are positive, with
$\langle B_p\rangle = 0.846$--$0.899$ (mean $0.868$, $72\sigma$
above noise) and $\langle A_v\rangle = 0.555$--$0.741$
($37\sigma$ above noise).  The minimum stabilizer expectation is
$0.5552$, unambiguously confirming topological order on silicon.

Every gate used in the paper is QCA-native; every clocking scheme
uses counter-diabatic driving; every stabilizer measurement is
compatible with the toric-code plaquette geometry.  The results
reinterpret three decades of classical QCA circuit design as
quantum circuit design, with no hardware modification.
\end{abstract}
