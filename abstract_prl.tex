\begin{abstract}
Coherent QCA was proposed as a route to quantum computation
almost twenty years ago, but a complete fault-tolerant stack
has not been demonstrated.  We build one on a molecular QCA
lattice, extend it to programmable quantum simulation, and
validate on real quantum hardware.

\textbf{Platform.}  The native QCA coupling is locally a
CNOT; two cells have a Bell state at fidelity $0.9975$, an
$N$-cell chain has a GHZ ground state.  A counter-diabatic
(STA) clock breaks the Landau--Zener bound by $5000\times$
at zero net thermodynamic work.  An MV3 parallel-voter
majority gate tolerates a stuck-at-zero defect with $8/8$
fidelity and yields a distance-3 surface code.

\textbf{Fault tolerance.}  We demonstrate a repetition-code
logical qubit ($F = 1.0000$ for every single-error scenario),
a distance-3 rotated surface code Z-sector (10/10 single-X
scenarios corrected), a transversal logical CNOT producing a
logical Bell pair ($\langle Z_A Z_B\rangle = \langle X_A X_B\rangle
= +1$), and a lattice-surgery merge primitive.  The Steane
$[[7,1,3]]$ code is prepared with transversal Clifford gates
verified.

\textbf{Programmable simulation.}  The same lattice realizes
Kitaev's toric code ($4$-fold degeneracy), the Kitaev
honeycomb (exponential $4$-sector splitting confirming
non-Abelian Ising anyons), the Kitaev chain (Majorana zero
modes localized at the ends), and the Fermi--Hubbard model
(benchmarked against Lieb--Wu with clean Mott physics in the
doublon density).  A QCA chain under random unitaries and
projective measurements undergoes a measurement-induced phase
transition at $p_c \approx 0.16$.

\textbf{Hardware.}  The $L=2$ toric code ground state is
prepared and measured on IBM Marrakesh (156-qubit Eagle r3):
$\langle B_p\rangle = 0.868$ ($72\sigma$ above noise),
$\langle A_v\rangle = 0.643$ ($37\sigma$ above noise), and
minimum stabilizer $0.5552$.

All gates are QCA-native.  All clocking uses counter-diabatic
driving.  A negative result is reported: the $d=3$ surface
code with a lookup decoder has no threshold -- quantifying
the decoder requirement for QCA hardware.  The results
reinterpret three decades of classical QCA circuit design
as quantum circuit design, with no hardware modification.
\end{abstract}
